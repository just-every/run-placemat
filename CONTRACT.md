# Placemat product contract

Target edition: **17 October 2026**. Placemat fills tables and runs restaurant guest relationships from first inquiry to the next attended visit. A US owner/GM switching from OpenTable, Resy, SevenRooms or Tock gets direct and distributed booking, the host stand, live queues, delivered guest conversations, deposits and prepaid dining, POS-connected profiles, retention and service economics. Eat App and Loman AI set additional expectations for automation and voice intake. This is the product being built towards; a missing implementation never narrows the destination.

## Product target

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Restaurant reservations, waitlist and guest management |
| Incumbents a buyer switches from | OpenTable, Resy, SevenRooms, Tock; Eat App and Loman AI |
| First buyer | The US owner or general manager of a 30–150 seat independent restaurant, with hosts, servers and a finance/marketing lead, operating in USD and US time zones; restaurant groups can add venues and shared guest relationships |

The whole job is to turn direct, Google, social, phone and discovered demand into feasible seated covers, protect scarce tables, keep service promises and earn repeat visits using actual guest and check facts. The product includes its own diner-facing booking/discovery experience and restaurant CRM. Acquisition, provider certification, approved POS access, US messaging registration, native clients and payment onboarding are work to deliver, not scope exclusions.

The existing contract had seven unnumbered guarantees. The Published foundations below give them stable IDs, preserve their capacity/authority/data guarantees and retain the first-release CSV reminder mode. Other clauses extend that foundation. Definitions state buyer behavior and acceptance through a screen, public HTTP door, download, command or provider event. [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md) lists every journey with the same status. Local tests do not establish live provider onboarding, real customer migration or native mobile acceptance.

## Operating rules

- The owner’s restaurant starts empty. Test fixtures stay in tests; real service, guest and provider records are created or migrated by the restaurant.
- Every booking channel shares one capacity ledger. A table, combination, experience ticket or waitlist hold cannot create another physical seat. A stale view must reconcile before it can commit.
- Existing preparation keys can read and prepare only. People approve exact plans; edits invalidate approval. New unattended transaction workflows require a person-approved policy enforced by a separately scoped execution service. An agent cannot approve its own proposal, grant access, alter policy or exceed a financial/compensation limit.
- Amounts use USD integer minor units with explicit tax, service charge, tip and refund allocations. Stripe owns payment credentials and money movement. Venue-local times carry an unambiguous offset and IANA timezone through booking, policy cutoffs, import and reporting.
- Transactional permission, marketing consent and sensitive guest facts are distinct. Unknown imported consent is unknown; a booking never implies a marketing opt-in. Accessibility needs and declared allergies constrain service decisions and are never inferred as medical conclusions.
- An automated send, payment or booking is complete only when its actual outcome is reconciled. A queued job, CSV, draft message or provider timeout is not a delivered message, collected payment or recovered cover. Failures have a named human next action.
- The public contract promises the target. The final Status table judges current code at the named commit, starting from the 16 October inventory audit and rechecking changed source/test paths; it is not a claim that missing provider work already ships.

## Published foundations

- **ACCESS-001 — Native sessions and preparation authority.** Private product routes require an active native owner, admin or member session, or an owner-issued unrevoked preparation key. Owner/admin operate the floor and approve plans; members read only unless the owner grants the product host capability in FLOOR-009. That capability permits direct assignment changes, staff service observations and WAIT-002 intake/party updates; a separate SERVICE-007 pacing grant adds walk-in/waitlist seating and reasoned overrides, never plan approval or venue administration. Only an owner person manages keys, move-in, erasure and backups. Every handler enforces authority and browser writes require the venue origin. **Acceptance evidence:** Call state, commands, downloads, import and backup directly while signed out, as member, admin and preparation key; refuse unauthorized writes and cross-origin requests; revoke a key and refuse its next read.

- **PRIVACY-001 — Public guest isolation.** Guests see published services and their own new-booking confirmation. Public availability and other guests’ confirmations never expose guest contact data, notes or private history. When BOOK-002 analytics is on, the booking page and widget ask once per visit before anything leaves for GA4 or Meta: nothing is sent before Accept, on Decline or without an answer, the answer is never stored, no provider script loads on the page, and accepted events carry no guest name or contact data, IP address, cookies, booking reference or reservation date/time. **Acceptance evidence:** Create two guests through the public booking door; inspect availability and both confirmations while signed out and prove neither response contains the other guest’s details. With BOOK-002 analytics on, book once without answering the analytics question and once after Accept; prove the first sends nothing and the second’s provider events carry no guest details.

- **BOOK-001 — Live assigned reservations.** Guests read current published availability and immediately create a booking assigned to a fitting table or combination. Full or concurrently claimed capacity returns a conflict rather than a false confirmation. **Acceptance evidence:** Publish a service, make three public reservations and race the final fitting combination from two guest requests; exactly one succeeds and the next availability read reflects it.

- **FLOOR-001 — Conflict-safe physical capacity.** Half-open UTC intervals, full-turn fit, starts on the service slot interval (15 minutes by default, configurable under SERVICE-004), distinct table combinations and cumulative service covers protect allocation. A physical table cannot be sold twice across overlapping services. Writes compare the floor version; stale plans require renewed review. **Acceptance evidence:** Book a combined table through HTTP, reject too few or duplicate tables and over-capacity arrivals, finish a turn and book a later one; attempt a clash across services and require refusal.

- **SERVICE-001 — Reviewed dated service publication.** An owner or manager configures venue timezone, positioned tables, a dated service, turn time and cover limit. A person publishes its exact prepared venue, service and floor snapshot; a later draft edit invalidates approval. **Acceptance evidence:** Create tables and a Friday service through commands, prepare publication, edit it, refuse stale approval and approve a fresh plan; only the published service becomes bookable.

- **PLAN-001 — Exact plans and accountable decisions.** Publication, prepared table-reassignment and reminder proposals preserve exact inputs, preparer, reason and the deciding person. A person’s own direct table move, swap or lock is not a plan: FLOOR-009 commits it at once with an audit row, so the approval path binds preparation keys and agent proposals. Preparation keys cannot approve. Changed inputs and physical clashes refuse approval; readable actor names identify people and keys. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare with an owner-issued key, refuse key approval, change a draft, refuse stale approval, then approve a fresh plan as manager and read its identities; prepare and approve a valid table move.

- **MSG-001 — Approved reminder CSV custody.** People can approve exact reminder batches and download them for their own sender. Exported rows retain approval attribution, render the venue-local time in US format (for example “Sat, Oct 17, 2026, 7:30 PM PDT”) and exclude no-shows and erased guest contact values. A CSV export records preparation and export, never provider delivery. **Acceptance evidence:** Book guests, mark one no-show, approve remaining reminders and download CSV with US-formatted local times; erase a booked guest and download again, proving the guest’s personal values are absent.

- **WAIT-001 — Staff walk-ins and waiting parties.** Managers seat fitting walk-ins, add a party to the service waitlist and seat it when capacity is released. Booked, seated, completed, canceled and no-show transitions are guarded against impossible state changes. **Acceptance evidence:** Run the Friday HTTP journey with online bookings, a walk-in, a waiting party and a no-show; seat the waiting party and refuse completion before seating.

- **GUEST-001 — Guest visit history.** Guest contact records, editable service notes, current reservation statuses and imported dated visit history remain linked to the guest, including cancellations and no-shows. **Acceptance evidence:** Book a guest, change its service status, import dated visits and read guest/state through an authorized session; update notes and find both current and imported history.

- **MIG-001 — Reviewed Tablein-shaped move-in.** Base import-kit accepts the documented eight-column guest/history CSV shape with a no-write dry run, exact fingerprint confirmation, atomic native/journal batches, durable provenance and undo that refuses later edited rows. This existing fixture is explicitly shape-only, not verified against a real customer export. **Acceptance evidence:** Upload the fixture through dry-run, reject a wrong fingerprint, apply, read the journal, undo unchanged rows and refuse undo after a staff edit while keeping that edit.

- **DATA-001 — Validated empty-destination backups.** Owner backups use Base portability with integrity, relationship and empty-destination checks. Restore never overwrites a working venue; corrupt archives fail without partial product writes and refusals give a plain-language next step. A restore brings back BOOK-002 analytics settings but never their GA4 or Meta keys, so the restored venue asks no analytics question and sends nothing until the owner enters the keys again. **Acceptance evidence:** Export a booked venue through the owner backup endpoint; restore into an empty installation, compare records, then refuse overwrite and a modified archive without changing destination data. Restore a venue with analytics on and prove nothing is offered or sent until the owner re-enters both keys.

- **DATA-002 — Guest erasure and restore fences.** Owner erasure removes live and import-custody personal values, suppresses known erased identities and fences known older backups. An isolated offline restore needs a trusted later erasure journal to learn erasures it has never seen. **Acceptance evidence:** Import, back up and erase a guest; verify guest and retained archive custody no longer contain personal values, refuse reimport and refuse a pre-erasure restore when given the trusted later journal.

- **SITE-001 — Runnable product and owner guides.** The run site built with rer build --site serves its homepage, a Run Placemat action opening /run and all listed owner guides at /docs. The installed root remains the Lobby and a fresh installation starts without sample restaurant data. **Acceptance evidence:** Build the run site and follow homepage to /run and /docs; open the installed root after fresh setup and reach empty restaurant operations. The homepage’s Run Placemat and Read the docs buttons and its Run it yourself command (`npx -y @runeditrun/rer dev just-every/run-placemat`) match `src/site/run.json`, README and /run exactly; run that command in a clean directory and reach owner setup.

## Target clauses

### Venue setup and service policy

- **SETUP-001 — US restaurant onboarding.** The owner sets legal/trading name, address, US phone, contact channels, logo, booking policies, USD currency and an IANA venue timezone. Setup previews a realistic service without creating fake production guests; Los Angeles and New York daylight-saving gaps and repeated hours require explicit resolution. **Acceptance evidence:** Finish onboarding for a 72-seat US restaurant, preview its booking page and create services across both DST changes; refuse nonexistent local times and disambiguate repeated times. Under ct scope51 (21 October 2026), completing restaurant setup saves editable defaults for new dated and recurring services: online parties 1–8 with inquiry above the maximum, 60 inclusive venue-local days ahead, inclusive 16:00 same-day cutoff, 30 elapsed minutes lead and 15-minute slots. Walk-in and waitlist defaults have no cutoff and zero lead. Every rule applies together; owner/admin may override party, horizon and lead with a recorded reason, never a cutoff or physical capacity. The owner acknowledges the exact summary, recorded with a monotonically increasing version, actor and instant; edits require a fresh acknowledgment. Existing services retain their own policies, and pre-onboarding installations retain their existing rules until the owner completes restaurant setup. Contact channels are the restaurant’s phone, email and optional HTTPS website; saving them claims no provider validation or send. PNG/JPEG/WebP restaurant logos are uploaded locally. USD is the edition currency. A transient preview uses the entered service and real floor through the current search/capacity engine and creates no service, guest, booking or proposal. Service creation and recurring generation refuse a DST gap and name its next valid local minute; repeated start or end times require an actual valid offset choice. Venue-local input resolves on the server and stored timestamps remain unambiguous instants. Restaurant fields, exact policies and acknowledgment export and restore under DATA-003 and DATA-001.

- **SERVICE-002 — Recurring shifts and seasonal schedules.** Managers define weekly breakfast, lunch and dinner templates, seasonal date ranges and special holiday shifts. They preview generated services, conflicts and capacity before publication; editing a template never silently rewrites existing guest commitments. Seasons and preview dates are inclusive venue-local calendar dates; dated exceptions replace the weekly shift, including opening a normally closed day. End time at or before start means the following local day. Nonexistent DST times refuse generation and repeated times require an explicit valid UTC offset. Preview shows physical seats, service covers and overlapping services, and publication binds the exact schedules, venue, floor, services and bookings. A template edit marks changed future generated services for renewed approval; only explicitly selected unbooked services can be renewed or closed by that reviewed batch. Booked services and historical services remain unchanged and affected bookings require the separately reviewed SERVICE-003 change workflow. Batches cover up to 31 dates each and successive batches cover a longer season. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a recurring dinner template and Thanksgiving exception, preview and publish a week, then change the template and inspect which future services need renewed approval.

- **SERVICE-003 — Closures and emergency service changes.** Managers close dates, rooms or arrival windows, hold inventory for private use and safely amend a published service. Affected bookings become a named outreach/reaccommodation queue with delivery outcomes and explicit refund decisions; closure cannot make them disappear. Owner/admin reviews each closure or relocation action. A closure message goes only to guests with recorded contact permission and covers only their own booking. Relocation changes a booking only after the guest accepts; unanswered bookings stay unchanged and visible to the owner. Refunds follow PAY-005: owner/admin authority, exact cents, recorded reason and no refund claimed before provider confirmation. Contact details/messages follow existing guest-data retention and erasure; every action records actor and time. Current closure restrictions use half-open intervals: room, full-service and private holds cover setup through cleanup across shared physical tables; arrival-window closures compare arrival instants. Existing reservations retain all commitments. Existing direct, swapped and prepared assignment doors refuse changes to an affected booking’s tables until a guest-accepted relocation workflow ships; an outstanding follow-up cannot be used to bypass acceptance. Follow-up outcomes are staff-recorded observations, never sends or delivery receipts. **Acceptance evidence:** Close a room with three future bookings; preview impacts, confirm the change, relocate one, cancel/refund another and track failed outreach for the third to staff ownership.

- **SERVICE-004 — Party and advance-booking rules.** Per service/channel rules control minimum and maximum party size, advance-booking horizon, same-day cutoff, lead time, slot interval and large-party inquiry routing. Staff overrides of party, horizon and lead-time rules require a permitted role and a reason and cannot violate physical capacity; pacing and cover-limit overrides follow SERVICE-007. Current online, walk-in and waitlist-seating channels each store their own rules. Horizon is an inclusive count of venue-local calendar days, lead time is elapsed minutes (zero disables the staff-seating lead check; public bookings always require a future time), the same-day cutoff is inclusive HH:MM in venue time, blocked starts are exact UTC instants, and slots align to service start. Current owner/admin managers may override party, horizon and lead-time checks with a recorded reason; public and preparation-key requests cannot. Large parties above the inquiry threshold are directed to contact the venue and are never falsely confirmed. BOOK-007 inherits the full inquiry workflow; BOOK-002, BOOK-005, DIST-001–DIST-004, WAIT-002, VOICE-001 and AGENT-001 inherit channel rules before their new booking doors ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure different lunch/dinner rules; try early, late, oversized and blocked public requests, then authorize a lead-time override and inspect its audit.

- **SERVICE-005 — Arrival pacing and concurrent covers.** Managers set covers and parties per arrival interval, total service covers, simultaneous seated covers and kitchen pacing by area. Public, widget, Google, social, partner, voice and agent allocations are refused at these limits with clear reasons. Staff bookings see the same limits as a warning and proceed past them only through a reasoned, audited SERVICE-007 override. Current limits are per service. Arrival intervals use whole elapsed minutes aligned to service start and half-open bounds; booked, seated and completed arrivals count, cancelled/no-show parties do not. Total service covers use those same statuses. Simultaneous dining covers count overlapping booked/seated dining intervals, with ongoing seated parties counted past their forecast. Each party counts once per arrival interval and once against each configured area containing its assigned tables. Whole-number limits are 1–500 and intervals 5–120 minutes; absent older pacing settings add no arrival or simultaneous limit beyond the existing service-cover and FLOOR-004 kitchen caps. Managers configure the policy at service creation or with an attributed service.pacing command; existing commitments remain, and assignment changes cannot increase an exceeded limit without a new reviewed allocation. Current online booking, walk-in and waitlist seating, status reacquisition, direct/prepared assignments and retirement replacements retain pacing and every hard physical/server guard. New widget/phone/distribution/voice/agent doors (BOOK-002, BOOK-005, DIST-001–DIST-004, VOICE-001, AGENT-001) inherit these obligations before shipping. WAIT-002 queue intake stays open at pacing caps, labelled “queued only; no table or time promised”; pacing is enforced when a person seats, books or allocates from the queue. **Acceptance evidence:** Set a 12-cover arrival limit and 48 simultaneous covers, book through two channels concurrently and prove the next public party is refused despite a physically free table, while a staff booking at the same moment receives a warning and needs a SERVICE-007 override. The reservation book shows covers against the limit for each arrival interval and marks a full interval, whose next public request is offered alternatives (BOOK-003).

- **SERVICE-006 — Turn and cleanup rules.** Managers set turn duration by party size, table, dining area, experience and service, plus cleanup buffers and last-seating rules. Availability fits the whole promised turn and reset; changes preview affected guests and preserve approved reservations. Current service, party-size, table and dining-area rules apply to every existing booking door. A table rule takes precedence over its area, then the matching non-overlapping party range, then the service default; a combination uses its longest constituent turn. Cleanup occupies the same tables through a separate cleanupEndAt, including a completed booking with a reset, and both the full turn and reset must fit service hours. Last seating is an inclusive offset instant. Reviewed policy edits preview each existing reservation, require renewed review if the service, floor or bookings change, and affect only future bookings; confirmed turns and resets remain unchanged. EXP-001 and EXP-002 inherit experience-specific duration and cleanup before publishing experiences; BOOK-002, BOOK-005, DIST-001–DIST-004, WAIT-002, VOICE-001 and AGENT-001 inherit full-turn/reset allocation before their new booking doors ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure 90-minute two-tops, 120-minute six-tops and a 15-minute reset; search last seating, book both sizes and verify adjacent turns and a policy edit.

- **SERVICE-007 — Staff pacing overrides with warnings.** Permitted staff (managers by default, hosts when granted) can knowingly book or seat a party past an arrival-interval, simultaneous-cover, area-pacing or service-cover limit, or into a blocked timeslot, from the reservation book, a phone booking, a walk-in or waitlist seating. Before commit the screen and HTTP response name each limit exceeded and by how much; the staff member confirms with a reason. The override is audited with actor, limit, excess and reason, flagged on the booking and timeline, and counted separately in pacing and cover reports (REPORT-002). Public, widget, Google, social, partner, voice and agent allocations never override and stay refused; WAIT-002 queue intake remains queued-only as SERVICE-005 states. No override can breach physical table capacity, overlapping turns or permitted combinations (FLOOR-001, FLOOR-003). Current owner/admin managers configure and override by their existing rights. An owner grants or revokes a separate pacing capability for an active FLOOR-009 host; it adds walk-in and waitlist seating with reasoned pacing overrides, never plan approval or venue administration. Each command rechecks the current grant. A native authenticated no-write preview names every limit/current/projected/excess and returns a five-minute HMAC review bound to the actor, command, exact payload, floor version and expiry. Commit needs that unchanged review, an explicit confirmation and a reason, and rechecks live fit. Review tokens are never stored or archived. Booking and audit retain actor/time/limit/excess/reason, the timeline flags the exception, and pacing and cover reports count and drill to overridden reservations separately (including later cancellations). Guest erasure clears linked override reasons in bookings, audit and retained proposal snapshots. Server caps/breaks, physical fit, access, joins and occupied turns always remain hard. BOOK-005 inherits future phone/concierge reservation-book creation; other new doors named in SERVICE-005 inherit these rules before shipping. **Acceptance evidence:** With a full 12-cover 7:30 interval and a physically free four-top, have a public request and an agent request refused. As a host without the grant, be refused. As a granted host, see the warning naming the limit and excess, confirm with a reason and book; read the audit row, the flag on the timeline and the override count in the pacing report. Attempt an override onto an occupied table and be refused. Configured FLOOR-004 concurrent kitchen-area caps are area-pacing warnings eligible for that same signed exception; server cover caps and breaks stay hard. Unrelated roster changes preserve existing reviewed kitchen exceptions; changing the kitchen cap itself still validates existing commitments.

### Booking and distribution

- **BOOK-002 — Branded website booking widget.** Restaurants embed a responsive, accessible booking widget on their own WordPress, Squarespace, Wix or custom website with venue branding, locale, party/date/time search and policy disclosure. The standalone booking page and embed share inventory and preserve source attribution. Owners can switch on consent-aware Google Analytics 4 and Meta Pixel/Conversions API events for search, checkout start and completed booking (with party size and source, never guest contact data). Before shipping, this capability must inherit SERVICE-005 arrival/service/concurrent/area pacing and SERVICE-007 warning, reasoned override and hard physical-capacity obligations; public and agent allocations never override. Embedding follows ct’s 20 October 2026 ruling: the owner alone manages an explicit list of up to 20 exact `https` website origins for the `/book?embed=1` widget (no wildcards, credentials, paths, `http`, `localhost` or loopback addresses, in any environment), and every change is audited with actor, time and the before/after list. Only that widget response names the list in CSP `frame-ancestors`, and it omits `X-Frame-Options`, which cannot express a list; an empty list answers `frame-ancestors 'none'` with `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, as does every other response, so staff pages, the standalone page, the queue and inquiry pages stay unframeable. The widget never submits on its own: a booking needs the guest’s own Confirm booking press. It accepts no messages from the host page, posts only its height to the listed origin and shows no staff link or data. Widget bookings use the public booking door with the online channel’s rules, turn/reset, joins, staffing, pacing and physical capacity, never an override, and record `attribution` `{door: widget, origin}` (the listed origin the widget reported: attribution, not proof); other public bookings record `{door: booking-page}`. The widget uses this installation’s own branding and venue name, US English with venue-local times in the venue’s IANA time zone, and the standalone page’s request terms, availability re-check and inquiry routing; both list each searched service’s online booking rules (party range, large-party inquiry threshold, horizon, same-day cutoff, lead time and arrival interval) before the guest confirms. Analytics follows ct’s 20 October 2026 ruling. Only the owner switches Google Analytics 4 (Measurement Protocol: measurement ID and API secret, optional `debug_mode` test mode) and Meta (Conversions API to the owner’s Pixel/dataset ID: access token, optional `test_event_code`) on or off; every change is audited with actor, time and before/after settings. Switching a provider off stops its events; its saved key stays until the owner enters a new one or removes it. Following ct’s 21 October 2026 ruling, the owner alone (as one of the owner’s own controls; no finance or staff role) can press Remove saved key for each provider: it deletes the stored GA4 API secret or Meta access token, leaves that provider’s other settings unchanged and is audited under AUDIT-001 with actor, time and `keySaved` false, never the key. While a provider is on without a key it sends nothing, the guest is not asked about it, and the settings say a key is needed; restores never bring a key back (DATA-001, DATA-003). The GA4 API secret and Meta access token are write-only: no state response, backup, archive, business CSV, audit row or log contains them (DATA-003 names the exclusion), so a restored installation sends nothing until the owner enters them again (DATA-001). While a provider is on and holds its key, `/book` and the widget ask once per page visit with Accept and Decline, naming each provider that is on and saying what is sent; the answer lives only in that page’s memory and is never stored or carried to another visit. Nothing is sent before Accept, on Decline or without an answer. After Accept the page creates a random visit ID, and Placemat’s server, never a script on the page, sends search (after each search the guest runs), checkout start (when the guest selects a time) and completed booking (after the booking commits) with exactly party size, booking source (`widget` with the listed origin, or `booking-page`) and the visit ID (Meta receives its SHA-256 as `external_id`), plus for Meta the browser user agent, the source page as `event_source_url` and the send time; never guest name or contact data, IP address, cookies, booking reference or reservation date/time. The visit ID is never stored on the booking or anywhere else. Sends run after the guest’s response with no retry queue, so a slow or failing provider never blocks or delays a booking. The owner sees each provider’s latest HTTP answer: GA4 never confirms an event (check DebugView) and Meta reports `events_received`; Placemat never claims a provider verified an event. **Acceptance evidence:** Embed on an allowed test website, search and book at 1440 and 390 widths, navigate by keyboard and verify the same slot disappears on the standalone page; observe the GA4 and Meta test events for the completed booking with no guest contact data, then decline analytics consent and observe none. Test events are observed where GA4 and Meta receive them, at Placemat’s outbound network edge, in each provider’s test mode (`debug_mode`, `test_event_code`): provider-sandbox evidence with the provider edge faked in tests, never hosted or production acceptance.
