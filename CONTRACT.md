# Placemat product contract

Target edition: **17 October 2026**. Placemat fills tables and runs restaurant guest relationships from first inquiry to the next attended visit. A US owner/GM switching from OpenTable, Resy, SevenRooms or Tock gets direct and distributed booking, the host stand, live queues, delivered guest conversations, deposits and prepaid dining, POS-connected profiles, retention and service economics. Eat App and Loman AI set additional expectations for automation and voice intake. This is the product being built towards; a missing implementation never narrows the destination.

## Product target

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Restaurant reservations, waitlist and guest management |
| Incumbents a buyer switches from | OpenTable, Resy, SevenRooms, Tock; Eat App and Loman AI |
| First buyer | The US owner or general manager of a 30–150 seat independent restaurant, with hosts, servers and a finance/marketing lead, operating in USD and US time zones; restaurant groups can add venues and shared guest relationships |

The whole job is to turn direct, Google, social, phone and discovered demand into feasible seated covers, protect scarce tables, keep service promises and earn repeat visits using actual guest and check facts. The product includes its own diner-facing booking/discovery experience and restaurant CRM. Acquisition, provider certification, approved POS access, US messaging registration, native clients and payment onboarding are work to deliver, not scope exclusions.

The existing contract had seven unnumbered guarantees. The Published foundations below give them stable IDs, preserve their capacity/authority/data guarantees and retain the first-release CSV reminder mode. Other clauses extend that foundation. Definitions state buyer behavior and acceptance through a screen, public HTTP door, download, command or provider event. [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md) lists every journey with the same status. Local tests do not establish live provider onboarding, real customer migration or native mobile acceptance.

## Operating rules

- The owner’s restaurant starts empty. Test fixtures stay in tests; real service, guest and provider records are created or migrated by the restaurant.
- Every booking channel shares one capacity ledger. A table, combination, experience ticket or waitlist hold cannot create another physical seat. A stale view must reconcile before it can commit.
- Existing preparation keys can read and prepare only. People approve exact plans; edits invalidate approval. New unattended transaction workflows require a person-approved policy enforced by a separately scoped execution service. An agent cannot approve its own proposal, grant access, alter policy or exceed a financial/compensation limit.
- Amounts use USD integer minor units with explicit tax, service charge, tip and refund allocations. Stripe owns payment credentials and money movement. Venue-local times carry an unambiguous offset and IANA timezone through booking, policy cutoffs, import and reporting.
- Transactional permission, marketing consent and sensitive guest facts are distinct. Unknown imported consent is unknown; a booking never implies a marketing opt-in. Accessibility needs and declared allergies constrain service decisions and are never inferred as medical conclusions.
- An automated send, payment or booking is complete only when its actual outcome is reconciled. A queued job, CSV, draft message or provider timeout is not a delivered message, collected payment or recovered cover. Failures have a named human next action.
- The public contract promises the target. The final Status table judges current code at the named commit, starting from the 16 October inventory audit and rechecking changed source/test paths; it is not a claim that missing provider work already ships.

## Published foundations

- **ACCESS-001 — Native sessions and preparation authority.** Private product routes require an active native owner, admin or member session, or an owner-issued unrevoked preparation key. Owner/admin operate the floor and approve plans; members read only. Only an owner person manages keys, move-in, erasure and backups. Every handler enforces authority and browser writes require the venue origin. **Acceptance evidence:** Call state, commands, downloads, import and backup directly while signed out, as member, admin and preparation key; refuse unauthorized writes and cross-origin requests; revoke a key and refuse its next read.

- **PRIVACY-001 — Public guest isolation.** Guests see published services and their own new-booking confirmation. Public availability and other guests’ confirmations never expose guest contact data, notes or private history. **Acceptance evidence:** Create two guests through the public booking door; inspect availability and both confirmations while signed out and prove neither response contains the other guest’s details.

- **BOOK-001 — Live assigned reservations.** Guests read current published availability and immediately create a booking assigned to a fitting table or combination. Full or concurrently claimed capacity returns a conflict rather than a false confirmation. **Acceptance evidence:** Publish a service, make three public reservations and race the final fitting combination from two guest requests; exactly one succeeds and the next availability read reflects it.

- **FLOOR-001 — Conflict-safe physical capacity.** Half-open UTC intervals, full-turn fit, starts on the service slot interval (15 minutes by default, configurable under SERVICE-004), distinct table combinations and cumulative service covers protect allocation. A physical table cannot be sold twice across overlapping services. Writes compare the floor version; stale plans require renewed review. **Acceptance evidence:** Book a combined table through HTTP, reject too few or duplicate tables and over-capacity arrivals, finish a turn and book a later one; attempt a clash across services and require refusal.

- **SERVICE-001 — Reviewed dated service publication.** An owner or manager configures venue timezone, positioned tables, a dated service, turn time and cover limit. A person publishes its exact prepared venue, service and floor snapshot; a later draft edit invalidates approval. **Acceptance evidence:** Create tables and a Friday service through commands, prepare publication, edit it, refuse stale approval and approve a fresh plan; only the published service becomes bookable.

- **PLAN-001 — Exact plans and accountable decisions.** Publication, prepared table-reassignment and reminder proposals preserve exact inputs, preparer, reason and the deciding person. A person’s own direct table move, swap or lock is not a plan: FLOOR-009 commits it at once with an audit row, so the approval path binds preparation keys and agent proposals. Preparation keys cannot approve. Changed inputs and physical clashes refuse approval; readable actor names identify people and keys. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare with an owner-issued key, refuse key approval, change a draft, refuse stale approval, then approve a fresh plan as manager and read its identities; prepare and approve a valid table move.

- **MSG-001 — Approved reminder CSV custody.** People can approve exact reminder batches and download them for their own sender. Exported rows retain approval attribution, render the venue-local time in US format (for example “Sat, Oct 17, 2026, 7:30 PM PDT”) and exclude no-shows and erased guest contact values. A CSV export records preparation and export, never provider delivery. **Acceptance evidence:** Book guests, mark one no-show, approve remaining reminders and download CSV with US-formatted local times; erase a booked guest and download again, proving the guest’s personal values are absent.

- **WAIT-001 — Staff walk-ins and waiting parties.** Managers seat fitting walk-ins, add a party to the service waitlist and seat it when capacity is released. Booked, seated, completed, cancelled and no-show transitions are guarded against impossible state changes. **Acceptance evidence:** Run the Friday HTTP journey with online bookings, a walk-in, a waiting party and a no-show; seat the waiting party and refuse completion before seating.

- **GUEST-001 — Guest visit history.** Guest contact records, editable service notes, current reservation statuses and imported dated visit history remain linked to the guest, including cancellations and no-shows. **Acceptance evidence:** Book a guest, change its service status, import dated visits and read guest/state through an authorized session; update notes and find both current and imported history.

- **MIG-001 — Reviewed Tablein-shaped move-in.** Base import-kit accepts the documented eight-column guest/history CSV shape with a no-write dry run, exact fingerprint confirmation, atomic native/journal batches, durable provenance and undo that refuses later edited rows. This existing fixture is explicitly shape-only, not verified against a real customer export. **Acceptance evidence:** Upload the fixture through dry-run, reject a wrong fingerprint, apply, read the journal, undo unchanged rows and refuse undo after a staff edit while keeping that edit.

- **DATA-001 — Validated empty-destination backups.** Owner backups use Base portability with integrity, relationship and empty-destination checks. Restore never overwrites a working venue; corrupt archives fail without partial product writes and refusals give a plain-language next step. **Acceptance evidence:** Export a booked venue through the owner backup endpoint; restore into an empty installation, compare records, then refuse overwrite and a modified archive without changing destination data.

- **DATA-002 — Guest erasure and restore fences.** Owner erasure removes live and import-custody personal values, suppresses known erased identities and fences known older backups. An isolated offline restore needs a trusted later erasure journal to learn erasures it has never seen. **Acceptance evidence:** Import, back up and erase a guest; verify guest and retained archive custody no longer contain personal values, refuse reimport and refuse a pre-erasure restore when given the trusted later journal.

- **SITE-001 — Runnable product and owner guides.** The run site built with rer build --site serves its homepage, a Run Placemat action opening /run and all listed owner guides at /docs. The installed root remains the Lobby and a fresh installation starts without sample restaurant data. **Acceptance evidence:** Build the run site and follow homepage to /run and /docs; open the installed root after fresh setup and reach empty restaurant operations. The homepage’s Run Placemat and Read the docs buttons and its Run it yourself command (`npx -y @runeditrun/rer dev just-every/run-placemat`) match `src/site/run.json`, README and /run exactly; run that command in a clean directory and reach owner setup.

## Target clauses

These clauses extend the published foundations to the complete category job. Each states the behaviour a buyer gets and the journey that proves it.

### Venue setup and service policy

- **SETUP-001 — US restaurant onboarding.** The owner sets legal/trading name, address, US phone, contact channels, logo, booking policies, USD currency and an IANA venue timezone. Setup previews a realistic service without creating fake production guests; Los Angeles and New York daylight-saving gaps and repeated hours require explicit resolution. **Acceptance evidence:** Finish onboarding for a 72-seat US restaurant, preview its booking page and create services across both DST changes; refuse nonexistent local times and disambiguate repeated times.

- **SERVICE-002 — Recurring shifts and seasonal schedules.** Managers define weekly breakfast, lunch and dinner templates, seasonal date ranges and special holiday shifts. They preview generated services, conflicts and capacity before publication; editing a template never silently rewrites existing guest commitments. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a recurring dinner template and Thanksgiving exception, preview and publish a week, then change the template and inspect which future services need renewed approval.

- **SERVICE-003 — Closures and emergency service changes.** Managers close dates, rooms or arrival windows, hold inventory for private use and safely amend a published service. Affected bookings become a named outreach/reaccommodation queue with delivery outcomes and explicit refund decisions; closure cannot make them disappear. **Acceptance evidence:** Close a room with three future bookings; preview impacts, confirm the change, relocate one, cancel/refund another and track failed outreach for the third to staff ownership.

- **SERVICE-004 — Party and advance-booking rules.** Per service/channel rules control minimum and maximum party size, advance-booking horizon, same-day cutoff, lead time, slot interval and large-party inquiry routing. Staff overrides of party, horizon and lead-time rules require a permitted role and a reason and cannot violate physical capacity; pacing and cover-limit overrides follow SERVICE-007. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure different lunch/dinner rules; try early, late, oversized and blocked public requests, then authorize a lead-time override and inspect its audit.

- **SERVICE-005 — Arrival pacing and concurrent covers.** Managers set covers and parties per arrival interval, total service covers, simultaneous seated covers and kitchen pacing by area. Public, widget, Google, social, partner, voice, waitlist self-join and agent bookings are refused at these limits with clear reasons. Staff bookings see the same limits as a warning and proceed past them only through a reasoned, audited SERVICE-007 override. **Acceptance evidence:** Set a 12-cover arrival limit and 48 simultaneous covers, book through two channels concurrently and prove the next public party is refused despite a physically free table, while a staff booking at the same moment receives a warning and needs a SERVICE-007 override. The reservation book shows covers against the limit for each arrival interval and marks a full interval, whose next public request is offered alternatives (BOOK-003).

- **SERVICE-006 — Turn and cleanup rules.** Managers set turn duration by party size, table, dining area, experience and service, plus cleanup buffers and last-seating rules. Availability fits the whole promised turn and reset; changes preview affected guests and preserve approved reservations. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure 90-minute two-tops, 120-minute six-tops and a 15-minute reset; search last seating, book both sizes and verify adjacent turns and a policy edit.

- **SERVICE-007 — Staff pacing overrides with warnings.** Permitted staff (managers by default, hosts when granted) can knowingly book or seat a party past an arrival-interval, simultaneous-cover, area-pacing or service-cover limit, or into a blocked timeslot, from the reservation book, a phone booking, a walk-in or waitlist seating. Before commit the screen and HTTP response name each limit exceeded and by how much; the staff member confirms with a reason. The override is audited with actor, limit, excess and reason, flagged on the booking and timeline, and counted separately in pacing and cover reports (REPORT-002). Public, widget, Google, social, partner, voice, waitlist self-join and agent channels never override and stay refused. No override can breach physical table capacity, overlapping turns or permitted combinations (FLOOR-001, FLOOR-003). **Acceptance evidence:** With a full 12-cover 7:30 interval and a physically free four-top, have a public request and an agent request refused. As a host without the grant, be refused. As a granted host, see the warning naming the limit and excess, confirm with a reason and book; read the audit row, the flag on the timeline and the override count in the pacing report. Attempt an override onto an occupied table and be refused.

### Booking and distribution

- **BOOK-002 — Branded website booking widget.** Restaurants embed a responsive, accessible booking widget on their own WordPress, Squarespace, Wix or custom website with venue branding, locale, party/date/time search and policy disclosure. The standalone booking page and embed share inventory and preserve source attribution. Owners can switch on consent-aware Google Analytics 4 and Meta Pixel/Conversions API events for search, checkout start and completed booking (with party size and source, never guest contact data). **Acceptance evidence:** Embed on an allowed test website, search and book at 1440 and 390 widths, navigate by keyboard and verify the same slot disappears on the standalone page; observe the GA4 and Meta test events for the completed booking with no guest contact data, then decline analytics consent and observe none.

- **BOOK-003 — Reservation search and availability alternatives.** Guests search by date, party size, area and experience and see honest nearby times or approved sister-venue alternatives when full, including accessible seating constraints. Empty and stale results explain the next available action without collecting a false booking. **Acceptance evidence:** Search a full Friday for an accessible four-top, select a Saturday alternative and complete it; race a stale result and receive a clear reselect path.

- **BOOK-004 — Secure guest modification and cancellation.** A scoped expiring manage-booking link lets a guest change time, size or contact details, reconfirm or cancel only their reservation. Show fee/refund consequences before consent and replace inventory atomically; changed links and replays cannot expose other guests. **Acceptance evidence:** Use a delivered link to move a two-top to four, fail an unavailable move without losing the old booking, cancel with policy acknowledgment and refuse an expired or foreign token.

- **BOOK-005 — Staff telephone and concierge reservations.** Hosts create future reservations for phone callers, hotel concierges and offline inquiries with source, contact preference and special requests. A permitted role can override party, lead-time or pacing rules with a reason (SERVICE-004, SERVICE-007). When a deposit or card-guarantee policy covers the booking, it sends the PAY-010 payment request link with a deadline. They search existing guests and quote live rules; booking confirmation reaches the guest in the selected channel. **Acceptance evidence:** As host, book next Friday for a returning caller, identify the concierge source, confirm by SMS and read the booking in the same floor schedule as online guests; book a six-top under a deposit policy and see the PAY-010 request delivered.

- **BOOK-006 — Booking holds and duplicate submissions.** Short-lived checkout/experience holds reserve scarce inventory across channels, display expiry and release on abandonment or failed payment. Stable request identity ensures reloads and retried submits return the same reservation; no guest can be charged for a lost slot. **Acceptance evidence:** Two guests start checkout for the final four-top, expire one hold, complete the other and retry its booking/payment request after a network interruption; find one booking and one charge.

- **BOOK-007 — Special requests and group inquiries.** Booking captures occasion, stroller/highchair, wheelchair access, dietary/allergy notes and seating preferences with explicit required versus requested distinctions. Large groups and private-dining inquiries have an owner, status, reply due date and conversion to a capacity-checked reservation. **Acceptance evidence:** Book with a required accessible table and requested patio, submit a 16-person inquiry, have a manager reply and convert it; verify guest acknowledgment of what is guaranteed.

- **DIST-001 — Reserve with Google.** Placemat completes eligible Actions Center partner onboarding and certification, publishes availability to Google Search/Maps, receives bookings/modifications/cancellations and reconciles inventory with channel attribution. A link alone is labelled a link until the actual end-to-end integration is live. **Acceptance evidence:** Through Google’s approved test/production partner journey, publish a venue, book on Google, change and cancel there, and verify matching reservation IDs and released local inventory; exercise feed rejection and outage recovery.

- **DIST-002 — Instagram and Facebook booking.** Owners connect Instagram/Facebook booking actions where partner support permits and publish trackable profile links elsewhere. Bookings preserve social/referrer/campaign provenance and real inventory; revoked profiles and unsupported account types receive actionable setup guidance. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect a supported profile, follow its reservation action at phone width, book and inspect social attribution; disconnect and prove existing bookings remain manageable.

- **DIST-003 — Diner discovery and partner distribution.** Searchable venue pages expose location, cuisine, menus, images, experiences and live availability; diner discovery and approved concierge/travel/marketplace distribution bring bookable demand. Partners use allocated inventory or the shared live ledger, with source fees visible and no assumption of inherited OpenTable/Resy network reach. **Acceptance evidence:** Find a venue by neighborhood and cuisine, book an experience from a partner listing and reconcile its source, fee and remaining tables; reject a duplicate partner callback.

- **DIST-004 — Channel inventory and attribution.** Managers allocate protected tables/covers by direct, Google, social, discovery, phone and partner channels, with release times, direct-booking priority and source/campaign attribution that survives modification. A channel outage cannot oversell shared physical inventory. **Acceptance evidence:** Reserve two four-tops for direct until 5 p.m., exhaust partner allocation, release it on schedule and inspect original source after a guest changes time. The reservation book shows each party’s channel and totals covers by channel (website, Google, Instagram, phone) that reconcile to its rows, including after a guest changes time.

- **DIST-005 — Guest accounts and repeat booking.** Diners can optionally keep verified contact details, favorite restaurants, manage upcoming reservations and rebook prior visits across devices, with saved preferences controlled per restaurant. Guest checkout remains available and identity never merges distinct people solely because they share a phone. **Acceptance evidence:** Book without an account, verify a diner account, attach only the verified booking, rebook a favorite on another device and revoke saved preferences.

- **DIST-006 — Verified diner reviews and restaurant replies.** Diners whose reservation was seated or completed (or matched to a POS check) can rate and review the venue once per attended visit on its Placemat venue page and booking widget; no-shows, cancellations and staff cannot review. Reviews show the visit date, averages show only above a minimum count, and the restaurant can post and edit one public reply per review and report abuse for moderation under published guidelines. Neither the restaurant nor Placemat removes or hides a review because of its rating, and review invitations follow CRM-005’s no-gating rule. **Acceptance evidence:** Complete a visit, receive the review invitation, submit a four-star review and see it with its visit date on the venue page and widget. Refuse a review for a no-show and a second review for the same visit. Reply as manager and see the reply public; report an abusive review and read the moderation outcome; prove a manager cannot delete a one-star review.

### The host stand and floor

- **FLOOR-002 — Rooms and editable floor plans.** Managers arrange rooms, indoor/outdoor areas, bars and numbered tables with shapes, seats, accessibility and joinable combinations. Changes are previewed against future commitments; retired tables retain history and live occupancy is visible without manual page reload. **Acceptance evidence:** Draw dining room and patio, retire a booked table with an approved replacement, book both areas and watch occupancy update on a second host device.

- **FLOOR-003 — Approved table combinations.** Managers explicitly permit which adjacent tables can join, their combined capacity, accessible fit and setup buffer. Allocation cannot combine distant or incompatible tables merely because their seats sum to the party size. **Acceptance evidence:** Permit T1+T2 but prohibit T1+T8; request six guests, verify the permitted join is used and reject a host attempting the prohibited join.

- **FLOOR-004 — Server sections and fair covers.** Managers create shift-specific sections, assign servers and tables, record breaks and cap covers per server. Hosts see seated/expected covers and rotation, while reassignment preserves the serving history and checks kitchen and server capacity. **Acceptance evidence:** Assign two sections and servers, seat uneven parties, put one server on break and move a table; verify cover totals, suggested rotation and attributed service history.

- **FLOOR-005 — Timeline and overdue turns.** A searchable day/service timeline shows arrivals, assigned tables, expected/actual seating and departure, cleanup, late arrivals and overdue turns. Hosts zoom or filter by room/server and resolve an overrun by moving the next arrival in one host action (FLOOR-009) or accepting a proposed replacement, never silently moving a confirmed reservation. **Acceptance evidence:** Seat a late six-top, extend its actual turn and inspect the collision with the next arrival; move the next arrival to a fitting replacement as a host in one action and read both histories. The host header shows seated covers now, covers due within a configurable next window and the average actual turn so far today, each opening its parties.

- **FLOOR-006 — Service statuses and handover.** Hosts manage confirmed, reconfirmed, arrived, partially arrived, seated, ordering, check open, paid, clearing, completed, late, cancelled and no-show states. Status changes carry timestamps and actor; POS observations cannot falsely mark a dirty table ready. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a reservation from arrival through paid and clearing to ready, correct an accidental state with a reason and inspect the next shift’s handover. The floor plan marks each table reserved, seated, mains, dessert, check dropped or needs bussing with a timer since its last transition, from host taps or POS signals (POS-007), and a legend names the same states.

- **FLOOR-007 — Busy-service search and shift handover.** Hosts find guests, bookings, phone numbers and table labels quickly, filter by service/source/status and see VIP, allergy, access and unpaid-policy alerts with PAY-010 deadlines. A handover lists unresolved guest requests, late parties and promised callbacks with owners and due times. **Acceptance evidence:** Find an arriving guest by phone suffix, resolve their access request, hand over an unpaid deposit exception and prove the next host can complete the promised callback.

- **FLOOR-008 — Concurrent devices and operational outage.** Host devices reconcile live changes, identify stale edits and show last successful sync. During an outage staff can read an encrypted, time-stamped service sheet and capture pending actions; reconnect requires conflict review before pending seats or reservations affect live inventory. **Acceptance evidence:** Seat from two tablets, disconnect one, record a pending walk-in and reconnect after its table is sold; refuse silent overwrite and reconcile to a different table. With the reservation book open on one device, make a booking from the guest page and see it arrive within seconds with a “new since” count and marker, without a reload and without overwriting a stale edit.

- **FLOOR-009 — Direct host table moves, swaps and locks.** A host or manager moves a booked, arrived or seated party to a free fitting table or permitted combination, swaps two parties’ tables, or locks an assignment, each in one direct action (drag on the floor plan or timeline, or Change assignment on the reservation) on tablet, phone and web. No manager approval is needed. At commit each action checks the live floor version, physical fit, overlapping turns, permitted joins and required access needs, refuses a move into an occupied or clashing table with the reason, and writes one audit row with actor, before/after tables, time and optional reason. A locked assignment is never changed by automatic allocation, reflow or agent proposals until a person unlocks it. The prepare→approve path of PLAN-001 remains for preparation keys and agent proposals only. **Acceptance evidence:** Signed in as a host, not a manager, move a seated four-top to a free fitting table, swap two booked parties and lock a third assignment, each in one HTTP command and one floor-screen gesture at 1440 and 390 widths; read the three audit rows. Refuse a move into an occupied table and into a table whose next turn clashes. Book a new party and prove automatic allocation leaves the locked assignment untouched. Prove a preparation key can still only prepare a `table.assign` proposal.

- **FLOOR-010 — Counter seats and unassigned bar pools.** Managers define bars and chef’s counters as seat-level inventory (a 10-seat counter where a two-top takes 2 seats, adjacent where required) and lounge or bar areas as unassigned cover pools with their own pacing and turn times. Counter seats are chosen at booking or assigned at arrival; a pool reservation holds covers without a table until the host seats it. Seats and pools share the one capacity ledger, turns and pacing with tables, and hosts can move a pool or counter party to a table under FLOOR-009. **Acceptance evidence:** Define a 10-seat counter and a 20-cover lounge pool; book five two-tops at the counter and refuse a sixth for the same turn; seat two adjacent counter guests and prove those seats are not sold twice across overlapping turns. Book a lounge reservation, seat it at assigned bar seats on arrival and then move it to a table, reading pool and table occupancy after each step.

- **FLOOR-011 — Late arrivals, holds and the no-show workflow.** Past a configurable grace time a booked party shows as late on the floor and timeline. A host (or the service agent inside a person-approved policy) logs each contact attempt, an outbound call or text, against the booking with its outcome: answered, no answer, voicemail, delivered or failed. When a guest says they are running late, the table is held to a stated time only if the next turn on it allows; otherwise the host sees the clash and the fitting alternatives. A person marks the no-show; the timeline records each step with actor and time, the party’s capacity returns to the shared ledger, the PAY-004 fee policy runs on recorded consent and the freed table is offered to fitting waiting parties under WAIT-004 with a stated hold window. A guest who arrives after release is seated at the next fitting table, never over a reassigned one. **Acceptance evidence:** Book a four-top for 7:15, reach the grace time with no arrival, log two unanswered calls and mark the no-show at 7:30 as a host; read the timeline with each step, actor and time; verify the table returns to availability, one PAY-004 fee is recorded and a fitting waiting party receives a table-ready text with its hold window and is seated. Text “running 15 late” for another booking and verify its table is held to the stated time; refuse an extension that would clash with the next booking on that table.

- **FLOOR-012 — Printable pre-shift sheet.** Before service a host or manager opens one sheet for the service date, printable from the browser, downloadable as PDF and readable on host devices. It lists the guests to know (VIP and regular status with visit count, occasion, declared allergies and access needs from GUEST-003, seating preference and the notes the viewer’s role may see), deposits and card guarantees held with amounts (PAY-002, PAY-003) and unpaid PAY-010 deadlines, the server sections with their tables and expected covers (FLOOR-004), the pacing limits (SERVICE-005) and the kitchen notes of FLOOR-013. It reflects bookings and changes up to the moment it is opened and shows when it was generated. Roles without rights (ROLE-003, ROLE-004) never see restricted notes or contact details on it, and erased guests (DATA-002) never appear with personal values. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare a Friday with a shellfish-allergic anniversary regular, an eight-top with a deposit and a chef’s-counter booking; open the pre-shift sheet as manager and print it to PDF; read each guest’s flags, deposit and section; change a booking and reopen to see the change and a new generated time; open it as a server and find restricted notes absent; erase one guest and prove a new sheet holds none of their values.

- **FLOOR-013 — Service notes and kitchen notes.** Managers attach free-text shift notes (for example “regular booked Thursday, seat in Ana’s section”) and kitchen notes to a service date. Every host device and the pre-shift sheet (FLOOR-012) show them, and edits keep author, time and history. The kitchen view of a service shows the arrival pacing limit and covers expected per interval (SERVICE-005), large parties, counts of dishes, add-ons or tasting choices that guests supplied at booking (BOOK-007, EXP-002) totalled by item, and each declared allergy and access need by table (GUEST-003) with no diagnosis and no statement that food is safe. Counts come only from guest-supplied booking data or manager entry and say which; Placemat never estimates portions. **Acceptance evidence:** As manager add the kitchen note “Halibut: 12 portions” and a shift note; as host open the floor on a second device and read both; collect a halibut pre-order on three bookings and see the item total with its source; record a shellfish allergy for the Table 12 booking and read it in the kitchen view beside the arrival pacing limit; edit the note and read author, time and history; open the kitchen view as a server and find only that server’s section allergies.

### Waitlist and cancellation demand

- **WAIT-002 — Remote and walk-up joining.** Guests join by QR, website or host intake, state party size, preferred time/area, access needs and contact consent, receive a secure queue-status link and leave or update their party. Duplicate active entries are explained; online joining can be paused independently of staff intake. **Acceptance evidence:** Join by phone QR and staff intake, refuse a duplicate, change party size and pause online intake while allowing a host entry.

- **WAIT-003 — Quoted waits and queue updates.** Hosts quote a range with timestamp and basis, distinguish reservation requests from same-day walk-ins and update guests when the estimate changes. Queue order accounts for fit and promises with a visible reason; never label a forecast as a guaranteed table. **Acceptance evidence:** Quote 20–30 minutes, delay a turn, revise the range and inspect the delivered update and guest status; seat a later fitting two-top while documenting the six-top’s retained priority.

- **WAIT-004 — Table-ready conversations and offer expiry.** A fitting party receives a table-ready SMS with reply/arrival window and status link. Yes, no, late, free-text and unanswered replies reach the host; expiring an offer releases its hold before the next party is offered it. **Acceptance evidence:** Notify two matching parties sequentially; let the first expire and have both reply yes together, proving only the still-valid offer seats and the other receives a clear response.

- **WAIT-005 — Future cancellation notifications.** Guests request alerts for sold-out dates, party sizes and experiences separately from the live queue. Managers control offer priority, eligible inventory and response windows; notify requests expire or stop on opt-out and fulfilled requests close automatically. **Acceptance evidence:** Request Saturday experience availability, cancel one ticket, notify eligible guests in the configured order and turn the accepted offer into one paid reservation. Cancel a same-day table and verify the freed table is offered to fitting waiting parties and notify requests in the configured order, and the book shows how many tables were offered and how many were filled.

- **WAIT-006 — Waitlist accountability.** Hosts see queued, offered, accepted, arrived, seated, declined, expired and abandoned states with wait quoted versus realized. Manual priority changes, no-contact parties and missed offers have reasons and owners; service close resolves every remaining entry. **Acceptance evidence:** Close a shift containing an unanswered offer, a no-phone party and a seated party; inspect dispositions, actual waits and assigned follow-up with no unresolved ghost offers.

### Delivered communications and follow-up

- **MSG-002 — Twilio SMS and WhatsApp connection.** Owners connect a venue’s Twilio sender with US A2P registration and WhatsApp business/template approval where required. Signed inbound/status webhooks attach provider IDs to guest threads; sender setup distinguishes pending, failed and usable channels. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect approved test senders, send a table-ready SMS and WhatsApp template, ingest signed delivery/reply events and reject a forged webhook; surface unregistered sender failure to the manager.

- **MSG-003 — Resend email connection.** Owners verify a sending domain with Resend, configure reply routing and receive signed delivery, bounce and complaint events. Transactional guest mail uses the restaurant’s identity, safe templates and recorded provider outcomes; authentication mail alone does not count as booking delivery. **Acceptance evidence:** Verify the domain, send a booking email to a controlled mailbox, reply to it and process bounce/complaint callbacks; show the guest thread and delivery state without leaking secrets.

- **MSG-008 — Consent and US messaging policy.** Store transactional and marketing permissions separately by channel, venue, purpose, source and time. Honor STOP/HELP, unsubscribe, suppression and quiet hours immediately across imports, jobs and agents; keep policy evidence and never infer marketing consent from a reservation. **Acceptance evidence:** Book without marketing consent, attempt a campaign, then opt in and send; reply STOP while another job is queued and prove the queued send is suppressed without losing booking management.

- **PAY-007 — Tax gratuity and service-charge disclosure.** Experience checkout itemizes base price, add-ons, discounts, US sales tax through Stripe Tax or configured approved tax rules, mandatory service charge and optional gratuity. They approve responses, review refunds and fee exposure, and export finance records for QuickBooks Online or the separate Pantry accounting product. **Acceptance evidence:** Open a test dispute, assemble the booking/policy/message evidence, approve a response and export its ledger to accounting; trace a rejected sync back to an owned task.

- **PAY-010 — Staff-requested card and deposit links.** When a host or manager creates or changes a phone, concierge, in-house or inquiry-converted reservation that a deposit or card-guarantee policy covers (PAY-002, PAY-003), Placemat sends the guest a scoped, expiring link by SMS and/or email to Stripe-hosted collection, showing the amount or guarantee terms, the policy text and a pay-by deadline in venue local time. Staff can resend, change channel or copy the link, and never key card numbers into Placemat. Until collected the booking shows as unprotected with its deadline on the floor, timeline and reservation list; a staff reminder task is created at a set lead time and an optional guest reminder is sent. At the deadline the venue policy either releases the table and cancels the booking with a delivered guest notice, or flags it for a person to decide. Paying before release marks the booking protected and links the receipt. A permitted role can waive the requirement with a recorded reason, and waivers are reported. **Acceptance evidence:** As a host, book a six-top by phone under a $25-per-person deposit policy; read the delivered SMS and email provider receipts carrying a link to Stripe-hosted test collection with a deadline; pay and see the booking marked protected with its receipt. Book a second party and leave it unpaid: see the unprotected flag and the staff reminder task, advance past the deadline and verify the table is released, the booking cancelled and the guest notified; replay a late payment callback and verify the booking is not resurrected without a person and the payment is refunded or flagged. Waive a third party’s requirement with a reason and read its audit row.

## Push further with service agents

- **AGENT-001 — Live voice and message booking agent.** A disclosed service agent answers phone, SMS, WhatsApp and permitted email inquiries using the restaurant’s approved menu/policies and current shared inventory. It books/changes/cancels only inside person-approved rules, obtains contact/policy consent and hands unknowns, urgent safety concerns and exceptions to people with context. Existing preparation keys remain unable to execute or self-approve; a separately scoped dispatcher enforces approved policy. **Acceptance evidence:** Call and message concurrently for the last accessible four-top, ask a menu question outside the knowledge base and request a fee exception; prove one fitting booking, a delivered confirmation, no invented answer and successful human transfer.

- **AGENT-002 — Cancellation refill through completed conversations.** The agent watches actual cancellations, table combinations and opted-in guest preferences, ranks eligible waitlist/Notify requests under manager-approved fairness rules, converses with each guest and completes a replacement reservation. Holds/expiry prevent competing accepts; incentives and contacts stay inside approved budgets and consent. **Acceptance evidence:** Cancel a six-top, offer to matching guests, let the first timeout and complete a second guest’s reply and booking; race a late first acceptance and report only realized recovered covers.

- **AGENT-003 — Honest waits from actual service pace.** The agent combines seated/arrival timestamps, POS course/check progress, staffing, kitchen-load limits and fitting tables to estimate wait ranges with confidence and source freshness. It proposes revised quotes and seating changes; hosts approve moves and promises outside authorized bands and managers own capacity. **Acceptance evidence:** Slow kitchen progress and extend a six-top’s turn, inspect the changed estimate and proposed move, reject it as host and verify no unauthorized guest promise or extra kitchen load. Each suggestion lists actions a host applies in one step, such as holding named tables to a later time, seating a quoted walk-in at a stated time or sending a waiting party who consented to texts an offer for bar seating, and shows the kitchen signal behind it; applying it records the host as decider.

- **AGENT-004 — Feasible seating and booking improvements.** Using demand, approved table joins, access needs, turns, staffing and pacing, the agent proposes explainable table allocations, protected direct inventory and shift adjustments. It previews affected bookings and expected covers/waits, never moves a committed guest or changes a published service without required person approval. **Acceptance evidence:** Provide a service with an avoidable stranded four-top, prepare two alternatives, approve one and reject a stale plan after another host seats a walk-in; compare actual utilization without breaking access constraints.

- **AGENT-005 — No-show risk and reconfirmation assistance.** The agent uses this venue’s observed booking/attendance history, lead time and reconfirmation outcomes to explain uncertain no-show risk and suggest contact or policy review. It never infers protected traits, auto-cancels a guest or imposes an unconsented fee; managers approve policy changes and monitor false positives. **Acceptance evidence:** Flag controlled risky/unconfirmed bookings, inspect cited facts and missing-data caveats, accept a reconfirmation task and decline a policy change; prove the guest retains their table and original terms.

- **AGENT-006 — Evidence-based guest recovery.** The agent reads verified feedback, visit notes, consent and POS check facts to draft recovery outreach, propose permitted compensation, arrange a return visit and chase unresolved follow-up. Managers approve sensitive replies and compensation; no invented apology facts, inferred allergy or unsubscribed outreach is allowed. **Acceptance evidence:** Receive poor feedback, inspect a fact-cited draft, approve a bounded offer, complete the guest conversation and return booking and track attendance; opt out another guest and prove no contact occurs.

- **AGENT-007 — Permissioned guest enrichment and next visit.** The agent proposes tags and repeat-visit segments from recorded preferences, declared occasions and spend with per-fact provenance, confidence and sensitive-field exclusions. Approved low-risk rules can run unattended; managers can reject/undo enrichments and preview any campaign before sending. **Acceptance evidence:** Process ambiguous notes and refunded spend, review proposed tags, reject an inferred dietary condition and approve a visit-frequency tag; undo it and verify downstream audience membership changes.

- **AGENT-008 — Measured reversible service experiments.** The agent proposes pacing, deposit or reminder experiments using accepted bookings, attended/recovered covers, quoted/actual waits, no-shows, refunds, guest complaints and POS contribution. Managers approve dates, policy terms, audiences and controls, can stop/roll back immediately, and see sample sizes, uncertainty and harms as well as gains. **Acceptance evidence:** Approve a two-week pacing experiment with a control period, record realized covers/waits and kitchen overload, stop it mid-service and inspect unchanged prior commitments plus a report distinguishing observed improvement from unsupported causal claims.

## Honest boundaries and separate products

Placemat owns reservations, restaurant guest relationships, dining experiences, host operations and their follow-through. It connects to Toast, Square for Restaurants, Clover, Lightspeed and NCR Aloha for orders, kitchen execution, checks and loyalty; those systems keep POS, menu fulfillment and payroll jobs. EXP-005 covers prepaid pickup experiences through that integration, rather than turning Placemat into delivery dispatch or a general POS.

Stripe is the restaurant’s processor. Placemat delivers the payment workflows in PAY-001–PAY-010 and experience clauses, including onboarding and reconciliation, while Stripe retains card custody, underwriting, bank settlement and processor dispute decisions. Sales-tax calculation uses the named tax connection and restaurant-approved configuration; policy/accounting decisions belong to the restaurant and its advisers. No missing provider approval removes a payment clause.

The restaurant CRM is part of this product. The general sales CRM remains [Orbit](https://github.com/just-every/run-orbit); general accounting remains [Pantry](https://github.com/just-every/run-pantry), which receives PAY-009’s finance handoff. [Crank](https://github.com/just-every/run-crank) owns general cross-tool workflow automation, [Uptime](https://github.com/just-every/run-uptime) monitors availability and [Oncall](https://github.com/just-every/run-oncall) coordinates responders. API-002 and OPS-001 supply the respective handoffs. No unowned category gap is absorbed here.

Google/Meta distribution and POS/voice partner access require actual approved accounts, certification and operational support. DIST-001–DIST-004 and the named POS/voice clauses include that engineering/commercial work. Placemat does not inherit an incumbent’s diner audience, but discovery and distribution remain target product work. It uses carriers and voice providers rather than operating a telephone network; agents transfer emergencies and safety issues to people and never present restaurant booking as an emergency service. Restaurant staff remain responsible for food preparation and the truth of menu/allergy information.

Two statements the homepage makes about what Placemat is are clauses here, so the page and the product are tested against the same words.

- **OSS-001 — Free and open source, MIT licensed.** Placemat’s source is MIT licensed in its public repository and Placemat itself charges no licence fee and sets no paywall, plan, seat or cover limit. Restaurants run it on their own computer or in their own Cloudflare account. Third-party providers (Stripe processing, Twilio messaging and voice, Google, Meta, POS and partner fees) bill the restaurant directly under their own terms; COST-001 shows those costs before a channel is enabled, and they are never called free or folded into a Placemat charge. **Acceptance evidence:** Read `LICENSE` (MIT) and the licence line on the homepage, README and /run; install with the /run command and run a complete service with no payment, plan or licence prompt; enable a provider channel and prove the cost shown is that provider’s own (COST-001) and that Placemat bills nothing.

- **BOUND-001 — Stated boundary: online ordering, gift cards and the POS.** The homepage says “Not in Placemat: online ordering, gift cards or the POS itself”, and the product says the same. Placemat sells no gift cards, stored-value cards or vouchers, takes no online food orders and is not the point of sale. A PAY-002 deposit, an EXP-002 prepaid ticket and an EXP-005 pickup experience are each tied to one named booking or event, shown as a balance against it and refunded under PAY-005; none is transferable, can be topped up or spends as a general balance. Gift cards, online ordering and check taking stay in the restaurant’s POS or its payment provider’s own gift-card and ordering products, and a Toast, Square or Clover loyalty program stays there too (CRM-006 syncs only identity and redemption status). The homepage names no gift-card provider, so the docs give the same line without naming one. **Acceptance evidence:** Read the homepage boundary line, this clause and the owner docs and find the same three exclusions and the same pointer to the venue’s POS or payment provider; search the staff app, public booking page, `docs/HTTP-CONTRACT.md` routes and exports for gift-card, stored-value, voucher and ordering surfaces and find none; pay a deposit and a prepaid ticket and prove neither can be transferred, topped up or redeemed outside its booking; a site test fails if the homepage names a gift-card or ordering capability this clause excludes, or this clause names one the page omits.
